# What protections exist for victims of stalking under EU law?

Victims of stalking are entitled to protections under EU law, particularly through the framework established by the Istanbul Convention and the EU Victims' Directive. The excerpts provide the following details:

1. **Istanbul Convention**: The excerpt states: "In line with Article 50 of the Istanbul Convention, which obliges parties to the convention to take the necessary measures to ensure that the police respond to all forms of violence 'promptly and appropriately by offering adequate and immediate protection to victims'." This indicates that EU Member States are required to ensure prompt and appropriate police responses to stalking.

2. **EU Victims' Directive**: The Victims' Rights Directive establishes minimum standards on the rights, protection, and support of victims of crime, which includes provisions relevant to stalking. The excerpt mentions: "The EU has already adopted a solid set of victims’ rights. The Victims’ Rights Directive includes the right to access information, the right to support and protection, in accordance with victims’ individual needs, and a set of procedural rights."

3. **Specialist Support Services**: The excerpts suggest that "specialist support services are required for victims" of stalking, indicating that there should be tailored assistance available to help victims navigate their situations.

4. **Legislation Review**: It is also suggested that EU Member States should review the use and effectiveness of legal provisions on stalking, as noted in the excerpt: "EU Member States should review the use and effectiveness of legal provisions on stalking."

In summary, protections for victims of stalking under EU law include prompt police response requirements, rights established by the Victims' Rights Directive, the need for specialist support services, and recommendations for reviewing existing legal provisions.
